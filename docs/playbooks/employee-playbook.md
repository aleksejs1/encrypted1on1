# The Direct Report's Playbook: Owning Your 1:1

[← Back to Playbooks](README.md)

> **For engineers, designers, and team members who want to turn 1:1 meetings into their primary career and sanity lever.**

---

## 1. Demystifying the 1:1: This Meeting is Yours

If your previous experience with 1:1 meetings was an anxious manager cross-examining you on tickets, or a boss canceling at the last minute, it is completely natural to feel nervous.

Here is the fundamental truth you need to internalize:
> **The 1:1 meeting belongs to you, not your manager.**

Your manager has team meetings, standups, sprint reviews, and planning sessions to talk about the company's projects. **This single 30–45 minute slot is the only meeting in the calendar designed entirely for your needs, your energy, and your career trajectory.**

### What a 1:1 is NOT:
- ❌ **It is NOT a performance review in disguise**: You are not being graded.
- ❌ **It is NOT a status check**: You don't need to report Jira tickets or explain every commit.
- ❌ **It is NOT a trap**: Admitting that a task is difficult or that you feel overwhelmed is an act of professional maturity, not incompetence.

### What a 1:1 IS:
- ✅ **Your personal unblocking engine**: A dedicated space to get systemic obstacles out of your way.
- ✅ **Your career steering wheel**: Aligning your daily work with promotions, skills, and projects that excite you.
- ✅ **Your safety valve**: Catching fatigue and friction before they turn into chronic burnout.

## 2. Before the Meeting: Preparing in encrypted1on1

The most common mistake employees make is sitting down 5 minutes before the meeting in a panic, trying to remember what happened two weeks ago.

`encrypted1on1` is intentionally designed as an **open cycle**: you can add notes incrementally as they happen.

```
                  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │      Between Meetings (Async)          │
                  │  • Log things you learned (Growth)     │
                  │  • Record small wins (Achievements)    │
                  │  • Jot down topics to cover (Discuss)  │
                  └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                      │
                                      ▼
                  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │       24 Hours Before Meeting          │
                  │  • Quick Mood & Workload check-in      │
                  │  • Name process friction               │
                  │  • Hit "Publish"                       │
                  └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                      │
                                      ▼
                  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │            During the Meeting          │
                  │  • You walk through your published side│
                  │  • Negotiate shared Action Outcomes    │
                  └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Step 1: Use the Continuous Logging Fields
Whenever something happens during your sprint, open your open anketa in `encrypted1on1` and add an entry:
- **Growth (What did you learn, discover, take away?)**: Solved a tricky bug? Read an insightful architecture doc? Figured out a new debugging tool? Click *"Add entry"*. By the time performance review season arrives, you will have a complete portfolio of growth that you don't have to reconstruct from memory.
- **Achievements**: Finished a complex refactoring? Shipped a feature ahead of deadline? Helped a junior engineer onboard? Add it here. Don't wait for your manager to guess what you accomplished.
- **What else to discuss**: Anytime a question pops into your head during the week (*"Should we migrate to TypeScript 5?"*, *"How does our team fit into the new company roadmap?"*), add it to your discuss list.

### Step 2: The 5-Minute Pre-Meeting Polish (24h Before)
The day before your scheduled 1:1:
1. **Mood & Feelings**: Select your current mood and trend. Look at the Feelings checklist (`excited`, `anxious`, `confident`, `overwhelmed`, `motivated`, `frustrated`, `grateful`, `proud`, `calm`, `stressed`, `bored`, `lonely`). Checking a tag is much easier than writing an emotional essay, but it signals to your manager how to approach the conversation.
2. **Workload**: Rate your workload (`Too much`, `Just right`, `Too little`) and trend (`More`, `Same`, `Less`). If you are drowning, flagging it here before the meeting gives your manager time to think about solutions rather than reacting on the spot.
3. **What's harder than it should be (Friction)**: This is your **highest-value field**. Name the things silently eating your energy:
   - *"Our Docker build takes 18 minutes on Apple Silicon."*
   - *"Reviewing PRs from Team B takes 4 days because their lead is in another timezone."*
   - *"I spent 6 hours this week wrestling with missing staging credentials."*
4. **Hit "Publish"**: Publishing your side gives your manager time to read your answers and prepare support.
5. **Private Notes**: Use the sticky **Private Notes** column on the right for anything you want to remember during the call that isn't for sharing (salary thoughts, personal health notes, private feedback draft). *Private notes are encrypted on your device and are strictly invisible to your manager and admins.*

## 3. During the Meeting: Driving the Agenda

Don't wait for your manager to guess what you need. Step into the driver's seat.

### Conversational Formula: Problem + Impact + Proposal
When bringing up an issue or blocker, avoid vague venting (*"Everything is chaotic"*). Frame it constructively:

```
1. Problem:
   "Our staging environment went down three times this week during testing."

2. Impact:
   "It blocked QA testing, caused us to slip our sprint demo, and created immense stress."

3. Proposal:
   "Can we dedicate 2 days next sprint to automate staging deployment via GitHub Actions?
   I need your help convincing the Product Manager to let us prioritize this."
```

### How to Talk About Money, Title, and Career Without Awkwardness
Many employees feel awkward asking about promotions or compensation. The secret is to **de-couple the conversation from demanding immediate money, and frame it around criteria**:

- ❌ *"I've been here a year, when do I get a raise?"* (Puts the manager on the defensive).
- ✅ *"I'd like to understand what the gap is between my current output and the Senior Engineer expectations. Can we review the competency expectations together so I know what skills I need to demonstrate over the next 6 months?"*
- ✅ *"My goal for this year is to step into tech leadership. Which upcoming initiative would be a good stretch assignment for me to prove my readiness?"*

### How to Speak Up About Burnout or Overwhelm
If you are drowning in work, silence is your worst enemy. Managers are not telepathic; if you silently work 12-hour days, they will assume everything is under control and assign you more work.

Say this directly:
> *"My current workload is not sustainable. I have 4 active projects and constant on-call alerts. If we don't prune my priorities, something critical is going to slip. Can we sit down right now and decide what we can drop, pause, or delegate?"*

## 4. Receiving Feedback Gracefully

If your manager shares constructive feedback with you, your lizard brain will want to defend itself, make excuses, or shift blame.

Instead, practice the **Feedback Receiver Protocol**:
1. **Breathe & Pause**: Don't interrupt. Let them finish their thought completely.
2. **Listen for Understanding, Not Defense**: Ask clarifying questions:
   - *"Can you give me an example of when that happened recently?"*
   - *"What would ideal behavior have looked like in that situation?"*
3. **Say Thank You**: *"Thank you for pointing that out. I hadn't realized that was how it came across."*
4. **Ask for Partnership**: *"What is one concrete thing I can do next week to start adjusting this?"*

## 5. Post-Meeting: Continuity and Career Building

### 1. Agree on Outcomes
Before leaving the call, ensure your shared agreements are captured in the **Outcomes** section of the anketa:
- If your manager agreed to resolve a blocker, make sure it is in their outcomes.
- If you committed to delivering something, add it to yours.
- During the next two weeks, you can check off items as you complete them.

### 2. Using the Period Report for Performance Reviews
When annual or bi-annual review season arrives:
- In `encrypted1on1`, click **Report** in the top navigation.
- Pick your date range (e.g. past 6 months).
- The platform decrypts every single entry you added to `Achievements`, `Growth`, and `Goals` and compiles them into a single, comprehensive Markdown document.
- You have an instant, evidence-backed brag sheet showing every technical hurdle you overcame and every skill you acquired.
